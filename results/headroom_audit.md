Generated by `python -m analysis.headroom_audit`. No LLM calls; all policies are scripted.

## 1. Public and private tenants are exchangeable

| overlap | public: shared-rule slots outside `public_rules` | private: same | max rule prevalence (public / private) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.067 / 0.059 |
| 0.5 | 0.460 | 0.494 | 0.067 / 0.059 |

`overlap` only changes the list the scripted `public_only` evaluator is told. Public tenants sample from all 40 shared rules, exactly like private tenants, so public experience and private experience have the same distribution. Every rule has prevalence of about 5%.

Under the interactive contract, the knowledge-free policy "always A, then correct" is already perfect, so no strategy can add anything. Under the one-shot contract, a Bayes policy fitted on 20,000 other tenants is the most any distilled strategy could reach; compare it with the tenant-only majority policy.

## 4. Plug-in learner placebo: Bayes table fitted on 24 public vs 24 private tenants (200 dataset seeds)

| contract | mean private − public | sd across seeds | seeds with abs. difference ≥ 5 pp |

| one_shot | -0.01 pp | 4.85 pp | 31.0 % |

The expected private-minus-public gain is zero because the two splits are exchangeable. A learner fitted on 24 tenants still produces swings of several points per seed, so single-seed differences such as the first pilot's +10.4 pp and −6.3 pp are within sampling noise.
